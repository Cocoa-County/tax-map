Start with a public question

The answer should not require a PDF scavenger hunt.

“What am I paying for?”

“Which district includes my property?”

“How was this assessment calculated?”

The evidence may already be in reports, resolutions, assessment rolls, and parcel data. taxmap gives an AI agent a repeatable workflow for connecting those records—with citations and checks, not just plausible answers.

From evidence to application

One workflow. A site people can verify.

The agent helps interpret the records. You review the model. taxmap builds the application from that reviewed data.

  1. Your sources

    Bring the evidence

    Documents, public links, assessment rolls, and parcel GIS.

  2. Agent + MCP tools

    Research & model

    Connect districts, parcels, rates, and formulas to citations.

  3. Checks + human review

    Verify & approve

    Check totals and joins. Explain gaps. Review public fields.

  4. taxmap build

    Generate the app

    Map, search, parcel panels, calculators, and source views.

  5. Approved deployment

    Publish & share

    Host the static site on GitHub Pages or another web host.

↺ New records or a new fiscal year? Update the project, review the changes, and rebuild.

Project files preserve the evidence and model at every stage. Building does not automatically approve publication or make estimates authoritative.

A new-city walkthrough

Imagine starting in Martinez.

Choose one documented assessment district or public question. Build a useful explanation, then grow as the evidence supports it.

  1. 01

    Give your agent a mission

    Once the plugin's skills and tools are live, ask for an app for your city and state. The agent derives routine settings and safely creates or resumes your project. Include a year, folder or focus if you want; a setup walkthrough is still available.

  2. 02

    Research the official records

    The agent finds adopted reports, resolutions, rolls and parcel GIS, and records its work through deterministic tools. It verifies the latest adopted year unless you requested another, and keeps progress in reviewable project files.

  3. 03

    Review the evidence and the gaps

    Check roll totals and parcel joins. Compare calculations with official amounts. Keep missing inputs, uncertain sources, and mismatches visible instead of hiding them.

  4. 04

    Approve a local preview

    Review the exact fields, sources and public explanations together, then open a checked localhost map. Useful partial coverage stays labeled, with source amounts unchanged. Publishing for neighbors requires separate approval.

Start the conversation

“Build a taxmap app for Martinez, CA.”

Start with the short CLI tools-setup request → Sign into one agent and let it follow SETUP.md to activate and verify the plugin. There is no blanket restart requirement; for Codex, fully quit and reopen the desktop app or exit and relaunch the CLI, then verify in a fresh conversation. The request above continues through research and a local preview, with one exact content review and questions only for genuine ambiguity, permissions or essential blockers. Host permission prompts remain separate.

Prefer more control? Ask “Create a new taxmap project. Walk me through the setup.” or use the manual CLI workflow.

Martinez is an illustrative workflow, not a completed app. Its sources, GIS coverage, districts, and bill mappings still need to be researched and verified.

Trust is part of the product

AI helps do the work.
It does not become the authority.

Your project stays yours

Sources, rolls, formulas, GIS, and explanations live in ordinary files you can inspect, version, and maintain—not only in a conversation.

Your community decides what is public

Review sources, publication rights, and privacy before deploying. Publish approved fields, not private correspondence or owner information by default.

Your app is portable

Publish the reviewed static export on GitHub Pages or another web host. Use the Python tools directly or work with an MCP-capable agent; an AI agent is not required for manual CLI work.

Rolls and county bills are authoritative. Derived outlines are approximate, not legal boundaries. AI-assisted research still requires human review.
